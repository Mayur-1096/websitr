A road to orbit, built from carbon and light.

Meridian is a fixed tether running from a floating equatorial anchor to a counterweight beyond geostationary orbit. Cargo climbs it. No fuel burns. No stage falls back to Earth.

Gravity does the launching for you.

Past geostationary altitude, centrifugal force outpulls gravity. Anything anchored to the tether above that point is flung outward, not down — which is what holds the whole structure taut.

The tether

A braided carbon-nanotube ribbon, four centimeters wide, running unbroken from sea level to a mass anchor 100,000 kilometers out. It has no moving parts of its own.

The climber

Cargo pods grip the ribbon with paired rollers and pull themselves upward, drawing power from a ground-based laser rather than carrying propellant.

Four stages, one ribbon.

Every climber follows the same fixed path, only the destination changes.

01

Anchor

The climber locks onto the ribbon at the ocean platform and begins its ascent under laser power.

02

Ascent

Six days of climbing through the atmosphere and into vacuum, gaining altitude at a constant rate.

03

Transfer

Past geostationary altitude, centrifugal force takes over and the climber accelerates on its own.

04

Release

The climber detaches at the point matching its destination orbit and coasts the rest of the way.
